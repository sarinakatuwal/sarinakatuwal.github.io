REFLECTION ON LEARNING

How I've grown

Reflecting on my career journey through the lens of career development theory — from fintech QA to cybersecurity GRC professional.

01

From QA to Security — Recognising the Connection

My five years in QA at F1Soft taught me to think adversarially — to find what breaks before users do. When I began security testing using Burp Suite and writing VAPT reports, I realised I was already thinking like a security tester. This recognition aligned with Holland's theory of career fit: my investigative and detail-oriented nature was naturally suited to cybersecurity, not just QA.

02

Learning GRC — Bridging Technical and Business

The Actualisation internship was a turning point. Applying OpenFAIR to model cyber risk and presenting to the CISO forced me to translate technical findings into business language. This reflects Super's career development theory — I moved from the exploration stage into the establishment stage, building real-world GRC competency and professional identity as a cybersecurity analyst.

03

What Surprised Me Most

I expected GRC to be mostly documentation. What surprised me was how analytical it is — quantifying risk with OpenFAIR, scoring Essential 8 maturity gaps, mapping frameworks against real controls. This experience challenged me intellectually and confirmed that GRC is where I want to build my career: at the intersection of technical security and strategic decision-making.

04

Where I'm Heading

My short-term goal is to complete my Master of ICT and secure a graduate GRC or security analyst role in Australia. Medium-term, I plan to pursue a CISSP or CISA certification and grow into a senior security risk advisory role. My career goal aligns with Krumboltz's planned happenstance theory — being open to opportunities (like this internship) while actively building skills that make me ready when they arrive.

Career Development Theory — Applied to My Journey

My career transition from QA engineering to cybersecurity reflects key principles from established career development frameworks. Super's Life-Span Theory frames my current position as the late exploration / early establishment stage — I have identified my direction (cybersecurity GRC), am gaining real-world experience through my internship, and am building the professional identity needed to transition fully into the field.

Krumboltz's Social Learning Theory explains how I got here: unplanned learning experiences in security testing during my QA role sparked curiosity, which led me to pursue formal cybersecurity study and seek out a GRC internship. Each step was partly planned, partly opportunistic — which is exactly how Krumboltz describes effective career development.

Going forward, I intend to apply Bandura's self-efficacy principles: building confidence through progressive mastery — first the internship, then graduate roles, then certifications (CISA/CISSP) — rather than waiting until I feel "ready."
