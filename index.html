Reflection on Learning

How I've grown

Reflecting on my career journey through the lens of career development theory — from fintech QA to cybersecurity GRC professional.

01
From QA to Security — Recognising the Connection
My five years in QA at F1Soft taught me to think adversarially — to find what breaks before users do. When I began security testing using Burp Suite and writing VAPT reports, I realised I was already thinking like a security tester. This recognition confirmed something I had not fully acknowledged before: my investigative and detail-oriented nature was always better suited to security work, not just QA.
02
Learning GRC — Bridging Technical and Business
The Actualisation internship was a turning point. Applying OpenFAIR to model cyber risk and presenting to the CISO forced me to translate technical findings into business language. This experience helped me move from simply exploring cybersecurity as a direction, to actively establishing myself in it — building real GRC competency and a clearer professional identity as a security analyst.
03
What Surprised Me Most
I expected GRC to be mostly documentation. What surprised me was how analytical it is — quantifying risk with OpenFAIR, scoring Essential 8 maturity gaps, mapping frameworks against real controls. This experience challenged me intellectually and confirmed that GRC is where I want to build my career: at the intersection of technical security and strategic decision-making.
04
Where I'm Heading
My short-term goal is to complete my Master of ICT and secure a graduate GRC or security analyst role in Australia. Medium-term, I want to pursue a CISA or CISSP certification and grow into a senior security risk advisory position. I have learned from this internship that being open to unexpected opportunities — while actively building relevant skills — is what creates real career momentum.

My Career Development Thinking

Looking back, my move from QA into cybersecurity was not a sudden decision — it happened gradually through the work I was already doing. Security testing, writing VAPT reports, and validating access controls were all part of my QA role at F1Soft, but they were quietly pulling me toward security without me fully realising it at the time. Moving to Australia and starting my Master of ICT gave me the space to make that shift intentional.

The GRC internship at Actualisation was the moment things clicked for me. Working on the Essential 8 gap analysis and presenting risk findings to the CISO showed me that cybersecurity is not just technical — it requires communication, judgement and strategic thinking. That is the kind of work I want to build my career around.

Going forward, my plan is to complete my master's, secure a graduate GRC or security analyst role in Australia, and then work toward certifications like CISA or CISSP. I am not waiting until I feel completely ready — I have learned that readiness comes from doing, not from preparing to do.
